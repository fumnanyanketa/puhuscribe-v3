# Hallway test for the welcome (job 15-onboarding, Done when 12)

Status: not run yet.

This is the sheet for the owner. The builder wrote it and has not run it. The evaluator checks only that the sheet is complete and that the counts add up. It cannot judge the people, and the job summary says so.

The test answers two questions about the welcome on the front door. Can a new person reach the first dialogue line in about a minute? Can they say, in their own words, what spoken and written Finnish mean in this app?

It takes about 40 minutes of your time for 5 people. Nothing is recorded and no name is taken.

## Before you start

You need the following.

- 5 adults, 18 or older, who have not seen PuhuScribe and do not speak Finnish well. Ideally they have lived in Finland for less than 18 months. Not you, not the native reviewer and not anyone who helped build the app.
- A phone and a link that opens the app. Use a preview link (the test database) or the live app once it exists. The link is your pick. Write which one you used in the box at the end of this sheet.
- A stopwatch (the phone's own is fine, on a second device or your own phone).
- Before each person, open a fresh private browser window on the phone with no account, and open the link, so the front door is on screen. Close the private window after each person. The next person gets a new one.

## What you say

Tell each person this before you hand over the phone. Read it as written.

"This is a test of an app, not a test of you. I will not record you and I will not write down your name. When you press the main button, the app makes a guest account with a random number and no name. It is deleted after 90 days without use. You can stop at any time."

Then hand over the phone with the front door open and say only this:

"Use this app as you like."

Say nothing more. Do not explain the app, do not point at the screen and do not read anything aloud.

## The stopwatch

- The stopwatch starts when you say "Use this app as you like." and the phone is in their hand.
- The stopwatch stops when the first dialogue line is on screen, with its play buttons.
- Write the time in whole seconds. If they have not reached the line after 120 seconds, stop the stopwatch, write 120 and take the phone back.
- A time of 60 seconds or less counts as reaching the line within 60 seconds.
- Give no help. If they ask what to do, say "Do whatever you would do if I were not here."

## The two questions

Take the phone back as soon as the stopwatch stops. Then ask both questions straight away, in this order, word for word.

1. "In your own words, what does spoken Finnish mean in this app?"
2. "In your own words, what does written Finnish mean in this app?"

Write each answer down as the person said it. Do not correct it, hint at it or say whether it was right. If the person says "I do not know", write that down.

## Marking guide

A person's answer passes when it carries the idea for its question, in any wording or language. Each question has one idea.

- Spoken Finnish: how people really talk in daily life.
- Written Finnish: how Finnish appears in books, forms and signs.

A person passes the answers when both answers carry their idea. A person passes the time when the stopwatch says 60 seconds or less.

### Spoken Finnish, answers that pass

- "How people talk to each other in Finland, every day."
- "The way you hear it in the shop or at work."
- "Normal speaking, not the school way."

### Spoken Finnish, answers that fail

- "The part where you speak into the phone." This names an action in the app and not what the form is.
- "A hard kind of Finnish." It says nothing about daily talk.
- "I do not know." Or an answer that only repeats the word "spoken".

### Written Finnish, answers that pass

- "How it is written in a book or on a form."
- "The kind you see on signs and papers."
- "The official way you read it, not how people say it."

### Written Finnish, answers that fail

- "The part you type." This names an action in the app and not what the form is.
- "The same as the spoken one but longer." It does not say where it appears.
- "I do not know." Or an answer that only repeats the word "written".

When an answer is in between, mark it as a fail and write a short note beside it. Note it in the free box below. Do not argue with yourself about it afterwards.
