# Job 20-a1-road: The 36-stop A1 road in the database, with the written dialogues checked, voiced and loaded

Feature `a1-road`. Needs a decision. GA. Flag: look closely (Finnish content, paid services, learner data, database access rules). Date: 1 November 2026. Builds on jobs 3, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 18 as merged on main. Runs in parallel with `19-redesign`, which draws the road screen from the interface in statements 5 to 7. Test label: `[a1-road N]`.

## Summary for the owner

1. The database learns the road: 6 parts, 36 stops, each with its name and "I can" sentence. Each stop holds 3 dialogues and 1 review lesson made from the words first met in that stop. That is 108 dialogues (the 102 Claude wrote on 1 November plus the 6 the pipeline drafted) and 852 lines.
2. A new import step reads Claude's markdown tables and turns each dialogue into the pipeline's draft format. It runs the free checks: Voikko on the written Finnish, the word frequency list, the spoken to written word mapping, and the grammar card checks. No AI call is made.
3. Azure voices read both versions of every line inside the free monthly allowance. My estimate is 50,000 to 80,000 characters against 500,000 free. Azure's free tier allows only 20 requests a minute, so each part takes about 15 minutes to voice.
4. Lines go live when the checks pass and you approve. You approve in writing, part by part (owner's call 3). Nothing is read by a native speaker before launch, so the real quality guard for spoken Finnish is your reviewer afterwards. Without the AI judge, nothing automatic compares the spoken and written versions beyond the word mapping (owner's call 2, about $0.60 to switch it on for the whole road).
5. The reviewer's fixes replace live lines later, through the review site that already exists. A fix gets a new approval reference. Withdrawn content loses its approval, as today.
6. AI cost is €0. Azure is €0 on the free allowance. GitHub minutes are free while the repository is public. The reviewer's time is the real cost: scaling job 7's 20 to 30 minutes per dialogue, 108 dialogues is about 36 to 54 hours (my estimate).
7. Your calls: how the word cards get their English meanings and "why" lines (call 1), the AI judge (call 2), how you approve (call 3), when the review lesson opens (call 4), and one spoken spelling rule (call 5).

## What it is

The 36 stops of the free A1 road, loaded into the database with all their dialogues, audio and word cards, ready for the road screen. Claude wrote the Finnish. This job checks it with the free tools, voices it, stores it, and sets up how it goes live on your approval and how the native reviewer's fixes replace lines afterwards.

It serves P1 (short dialogues), P2 (the review lesson and the forms feeding review), P3 (each stop tied to an "I can" sentence) and P5 (both registers with audio).

### The database (can start now)

1. **Parts and stops.** A migration creates `road_parts` (number, English title) and `road_stops` (number, slug, part number, English name, "I can" sentence, planned dialogues). It loads 6 parts and 36 stops. Names and sentences equal `docs/curriculum-a1.md` and the `**I can:**` lines of `content/written/part-1.md` to `part-6.md`, for example stop 4 is "Numbers, prices and paying", part 1 is "First words". The migration is generated by a script from those files, and a test fails when the committed file differs from the script's output. Both tables allow stops past 36 and parts past 6, so later legs (A2 and beyond) are added by a new migration.
2. **Stops link to units.** `units` gets a nullable `stop_number`, unique, referencing `road_stops`, and equal to `position` when set. The test fixture units (positions 901 and 902) keep it empty. The existing content rules are unchanged.
3. **Word card texts in `forms`.** `forms` gets `meaning_en` (what this word form means in context, such as "those") and `lemma_meaning_en` (what its dictionary form means, such as "that"). Approving a form needs both, each non empty, at most 60 characters and without control characters. Approved forms stay frozen and withdrawn ones lose their approval exactly as before. `src/lib/database.types.ts` is the Supabase CLI's exact output. The migration runs on a database that holds no approved forms, which is every database today, and a pgTAP test proves the new approval check refuses a form missing either text.
4. **Access rules for every new table.** The migration revokes all access on `road_parts` and `road_stops` from `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`, grants `select` to `authenticated` only, turns on row level security and adds one read policy each. These tables hold English only: no Finnish, no learner data. pgTAP (`supabase/tests/database/a1_road.test.sql`) proves: `anon` and `service_role` read nothing; a learner reads every row; a learner cannot insert, update or delete; each grant and each policy has its own failing case, shown by removing it on purpose and watching only its own test go red.
5. **The road interface, `public.road()`.** One function, no arguments, `security invoker`, empty search path, callable only by `authenticated`, writing nothing. It returns one row per stop in stop order, always all 36, including stops with no published content yet:

   | Column | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | `stop_number`, `stop_slug`, `title_en`, `can_do_en` | The stop and its "I can" sentence |
   | `part_number`, `part_title_en` | Its part |
   | `dialogues_total` | Planned dialogues (3) |
   | `dialogues_ready` | Dialogues the caller can read (approved, in an approved stop) |
   | `dialogues_done` | Distinct dialogues of the stop in which the caller has a session with an end time |
   | `review_forms_total` | The stop's new forms the caller can read |
   | `review_forms_reviewed` | Of those, forms with at least one of the caller's review logs |
   | `lessons_total` | 4 |
   | `lessons_done` | `dialogues_done`, plus 1 when `review_forms_total` is above 0 and `review_forms_reviewed` equals it |
   | `live` | The stop is approved and `dialogues_ready` equals `dialogues_total` |

   pgTAP with two learners proves each column, that only the caller's own sessions and logs count, that an unapproved dialogue is never counted ready, and that the function goes red if it is made `security definer`. It returns counts only, never a word, a Finnish line or another learner's id.
6. **The review lesson's words, `public.stop_review_forms(p_stop_number)`.** Same safety as statement 5. It returns the form ids of the stop's new forms, in order of first appearance, that the caller holds a card for. A form the learner has not met in a finished session is never returned (nothing unmet is reviewed). pgTAP covers a met form, an unmet form, a form first met in an earlier stop, and another learner's card. The review lesson is made only of data here, no new table: no session type and no learner column are added.
7. **`GET /api/road`.** The Worker route the screen calls. It reads `road()` with the learner's own token and the publishable key, answers 401 without an account, changes nothing, and returns the statement 5 columns and nothing else (no user id, no card numbers). A fault answers 503 with no detail. Worker unit tests with a fake database cover each case, including that the answer holds no id. **The words "answers 401 without an account" are replaced by statement 29 (manager's amendment, 1 November 2026): with no sign in the route answers 200 with no progress.**

### The import path (runs in GitHub on Linux with Voikko)

8. **Reading the markdown.** `python scripts/generate/road_import.py` reads `docs/curriculum-a1.md` and the six part files. It finds 36 stops and 102 dialogues of exactly 8 lines, each row with a speaker, a spoken text, a written text and an English text. It refuses, naming the file, stop and line, a row with an empty cell, a stray pipe, a line number out of order, a stop without its **I can:** line or a dialogue count that is not 3 once the six earlier drafts are counted. All text is plain text and is escaped wherever it is shown.
9. **Stop settings and the brief.** `content/road/stops.json` holds, for each stop, its unit slug, rank cap, grammar ids and the extra base forms it allows. Stops 1 to 3 keep the slugs `meeting-people`, `ordering-coffee` and `grocery-store`; their six earlier briefs and drafts take the road's name and "I can" sentence in their `unit` block only. The brief format gains one optional field, `source` (`ai` by default, `written` for these). For `written`, `role_en` may be left out and `situation_en` is the scene sentence from the markdown. A speaker file, `content/road/speakers.json`, gives each named speaker one of job 7's three Azure voices, the same voice every time, and no two speakers of a dialogue share one. Test: the existing brief tests pass unchanged.
10. **A draft for every dialogue, with no AI.** The importer writes `content/drafts/<slug>/draft.json` in `puhuscribe-draft-1` format for the 102 new dialogues: lines, written and spoken tokens, Voikko's reading for each written word, forms, and `providers` set to `{"ai": "written", "judge": "off"}` (or `"on"`, statement 15). Slugs are the English dialogue title in kebab case, unique across all 108. Pytest runs the importer with the network switched off and with `AI_PROVIDER` unset, and it succeeds. A test finds no import of `anthropic` outside `providers/ai.py`.
11. **The spoken to written mapping.** Each spoken word is paired with the written word it renders, in order. A pair is accepted when the words are equal ignoring case, or the pair is in `content/road/spoken-forms.tsv` (for example `mä, minä`, `noi, nuo`, `joo, kyllä`, `moikka, näkemiin`), or a documented ending rule applies (dropped final vowel or consonant, `-ii` for `-ia`, `-oo` for `-oa`, `-ks` for `-ko`). Any other pair fails the line with both words named. A written word no spoken word renders fails the line. A spoken only word is accepted only for a filler with no written counterpart; a spoken pronoun (mä, sä, me, te) must pair with a written pronoun, so a line whose written version drops it gets the pronoun added. A spoken word glued to `sä` after a verb (about 37 words today, such as `Ootsä` and `Puhutsä`) is split into two words (`Oot sä`), by one documented rule that logs each change, because every token links to one form. Pytest has one planted failure for each rule.
12. **The free checks run on all 108.** Voikko knows every written word; each word's tokens appear in order and cover the line; links are one to one; level fit holds; grammar cards match Voikko; each "why" follows the rules (job 7 statements 7 to 11 and 21, unchanged code and unchanged `content/checks/thresholds.json`, byte for byte as on main). `content/road/road-report.md` lists, per stop, each check's rate, the spoken only words, the pairs accepted by table, the extras of statement 13, and the number of forms. `python scripts/generate/pipeline.py check-committed` passes on every pull request with all 108 drafts. A new test fails when a committed road draft differs from what the importer makes from the markdown (audio fields apart).
13. **Level fit for written content.** `GRAMMAR_CATALOGUE` gains one entry for each item of the curriculum's grammar column that it lacks (for example plural nominative, genitive, illative, past tense, conditional, imperative, comparison, possessive endings, ordinal numbers, numbers to 100), each a test on Voikko's own attributes. A stop's list is its own entries plus every earlier stop's. A form that is outside the list is fixed by changing the line, or recorded in `content/road/allowed-extras.json` with a reason in plain English. The rank cap per part is the lowest one that leaves each stop with at most 15 words on its extra list. Pytest proves a planted out of list form and a planted out of level word each fail, and that the report prints the count of recorded extras per part, so you see what was allowed.
14. **Only Claude's content is repaired, never a check.** Every change made after a failing run is a change to the markdown tables or a road file, and each is listed in `content/road/fixes.md` with before, after and the reason. No threshold, check or test is edited to make a draft pass. The builder stops and tells the manager when, after three repair rounds, more than 5 percent of a part's lines still fail.
15. **The register judge is off unless the owner turns it on.** With it off, each draft records `"judge": "off"`, `check-committed` prints "register judge: not run" for it, and the register check reports "not run", never "passed". A draft that says `"judge": "on"` but lacks a verdict for any line still fails, as today. When it is on, the existing judge module runs on every line of the part, every call is logged with its cost, the golden set is run once first and must pass (job 7 statement 11), and a run that would pass $2 stops. Pytest covers off, on and on with a missing verdict. The approval file (statement 20) names which state each part was approved in.
16. **Word cards filled without paid calls.** Per owner's call 1 (recommended A): `content/road/glossary.tsv` gives each dictionary form its English meaning, and `content/road/word-notes.tsv` gives each form that is not an unchanged dictionary form its own meaning and, when its spoken shape differs from its written shape or it is not the dictionary form, a one line "why". Voikko gives the dictionary form. A form that is a dictionary form and sounds the same in speech gets a fixed line from a table in code ("This is the dictionary form. People say it the same way."). The importer writes `needed-notes.tsv` listing every form still missing a text with its line and English meaning, so a working session can fill it. Tests: every form of all 108 drafts has both meanings and a "why" within the existing rules (140 characters, no foreign grammar term, no mnemonic); all forms of one dictionary form share one `lemma_meaning_en`; one form id carries the same texts in every draft that has it. **The lemma rule.** A form's `lemma` is Voikko's base form of the written word the spoken word is paired with, because Voikko cannot analyse spoken Finnish. So spoken `noi` pairs with written `nuo`, and both `noi` and `toi` show the dictionary form `tuo`. A pytest on line 2 of dialogue 4.1 (`Paljonks noi mansikat maksaa?`) proves it with real Voikko.
17. **The six earlier drafts keep their lines and audio.** Their lines, tokens, `why` lines and audio files are byte for byte as committed. They gain `meaning_en` and `lemma_meaning_en` for their forms from the road files, and their `unit` block changes as in statement 9. Their review manifests are regenerated because form fingerprints now include the two meanings; no reviewer file exists for them yet (`content/reviews/` holds only its README), which a test confirms before it regenerates.

### Voices

18. **Azure voices inside the free allowance, resumable.** `providers/voice.py` stays the only file that calls a voice service and gains: at most 20 requests in any 60 seconds (Azure's free tier limit), a retry with waiting on HTTP 429 and server errors, and a skip of any clip that already exists with a matching hash of its text and voice. Before any call, `road_import.py count-characters` prints the exact number of characters to voice for a part and for the road, and the run refuses to start when this month's logged characters plus the new ones would pass 450,000 (90 percent of the allowance). The exact total goes in `build.md`. Pytest with a fake clock proves the 20 per minute limit, the retry, the skip and the refusal. The Azure key reaches only the voice step of the workflow.
19. **Voicing by part, one copy of each clip.** A manual workflow `.github/workflows/a1-road.yml` (task `import`, `voice` or `review-pages`; input part 1 to 6) follows the content pipeline workflow's shape: actions pinned to a full commit, no `npm ci` next to a key, a secret check on every uploaded file, `contents: read`. Voicing a part is about 272 clips at 20 a minute, so about 15 minutes; the job's timeout is 45. Clips are committed once, under `content/drafts/<slug>/audio/`, and copied to `public/audio/` only at publish (statement 21). Review pages for these dialogues are not built until a batch is sent (statement 25). `build.md` reports the total audio size. If the audio plus migrations pass 100 MB, the builder stops and tells the manager.

### Publishing under the owner's approval

20. **The owner's approval is the approval reference.** `content/approvals/part-<n>.json` (format `puhuscribe-owner-approval-1`) holds the owner's own written approval line word for word with its date, the part, the judge state, and for each dialogue of the part its fingerprint (a SHA-256 of every line's three texts and every form's texts). Only the manager adds this file, on the owner's written instruction. `publish` for written drafts refuses, naming the reason, when the file is missing, the dialogue is not listed, its fingerprint differs from the current draft, a check fails, the audio is missing or fake, or the part has a failing dialogue. Each `approval_ref` is the file path plus `#<dialogue-slug>/<item id>`, so every reference is new. Pytest covers each refusal and that a changed line voids its dialogue's approval. The existing reviewer path for AI drafts is unchanged.
21. **108 publish migrations, in order.** The publisher writes one migration per dialogue, with explicit timestamps ordered by stop and dialogue and later than every migration on main, so the three dialogues of a stop apply in order. The unit row is made from `road_stops` (slug, position, name, sentence, stop number) and the stop is approved only inside the migration of its last dialogue, so a learner never sees a stop with a dialogue missing. Each migration does nothing when its dialogue is already published, so the workflow's second run changes nothing (job 7 statement 16). Forms follow the order in `docs/data-model.md`. A pytest checks that the 108 migrations match what the publisher makes from the drafts and approval files, and that total size is reported (stop line 25 MB). Audio is copied to `public/audio/<key>` with the existing key rule, and `tests/content-audio.test.ts` passes for all of it.
22. **A learner reads the whole road.** After the migrations, a pgTAP test acting as a learner reads 36 approved units, 108 approved dialogues, 852 approved lines with both texts, the gloss and both audio keys, every word linked to an approved form with a name, a "why" and both meanings, and `road()` shows 36 live stops. A draft line added inside the test is not readable. Both tests go red when the published data is changed on purpose.
23. **Everything already built still works with the road in place.** The real content now sits in every throwaway database. The builder first lists the browser tests that assumed the fixture was the only content. Where one did, only its setup changes (it hides the road's units in the throwaway database, as the empty content pass already does), never an assertion. The empty content pass still shows "Today's session is not ready yet." Reads that list lines, tokens, forms or cards (`worker/patterns.ts`, `worker/learner-db.ts`) are checked against Supabase's 1,000 row cap (`max_rows` in `supabase/config.toml`; the hosted setting was not checked): with a learner who has met all 108 dialogues, today's session, the review queue and the patterns panel return complete answers, and a read that was silently cut is paged or filtered, with a test that fails without the fix. All tests from jobs 1 to 18 stay green and unchanged.

### After launch

24. **A reviewer fix replaces a live line.** `pipeline.py road-next-round --draft content/drafts/<slug>` reads the reviewer's file in `content/reviews/<slug>/` with job 7's refusal rules, writes each accepted fix into the exact markdown cell, re-imports that dialogue, checks it, re-voices only the changed lines, and writes `supabase/migrations/<timestamp>_fix_<slug>_round_<n>.sql`. In one transaction it sets the dialogue back to draft, the changed lines to draft, replaces their words and audio keys, approves the new forms, then approves the lines and the dialogue with new references of the form `content/reviews/<slug>/round-<n>.json#line-<k>`. A pgTAP test on a fixture proves: a learner never reads a half changed dialogue; the old reference is in `private.approval_history` and approving with it again is refused; a line changed by a fix cannot be approved with the owner's old reference; retiring or setting back to draft still clears the approval. The step makes no AI call: new forms that need texts stop the command with a list for `needed-notes.tsv`.
25. **Review pages on demand, same review site.** `pipeline.py review-pages --part <n>` writes round 1 pages for a part's dialogues, showing for each form the two meanings next to the base form, name and "why"; the form fingerprint covers them. The manager adds slugs to `review-site/dialogues.json` on the owner's instruction, and the owner sends the link as today. A reviewer who approves a live line changes nothing in the database; the file in `content/reviews/` is the record that a native speaker saw it.

## Threat model (GA)

**Worst thing a malicious person could do here.**
- Get wrong or harmful Finnish in front of every learner, because launch happens before a native speaker reads it. Routes: edit the markdown after approval, swap an audio file, edit a publish migration, or write a fake approval file. Defences: the approval file binds each dialogue to a fingerprint, so a changed line voids its approval (20); audio names carry their own hash and a test checks them (job 7); publishing reaches a database only through an owner approved merge; only the manager adds approval files, on the owner's written line, which the summary quotes; the reviewer then reads everything and fixes replace lines (24).
- Steal the Azure key (or the Anthropic key, if the judge is on) and run up a bill. Defences: keys only in GitHub secrets and only in the one step that needs them; a workflow that runs only when started by hand; the free tier cannot bill; the character guard stops at 450,000 (18); the owner's €10 alert on the speech account (factory plan decision 7).
- Plant script or SQL in a markdown cell. Defences: text is escaped wherever shown; the publisher refuses text holding its code block tag and escapes every quote (job 7); the review page prints text as text.
- Read other learners' progress through `road()`. Defence: `security invoker`, no argument, own rows only, pgTAP with two learners (5, 6).

**Data we must protect.** The two API keys. The integrity of the approval files. No learner data is read, sent or stored by the import, the voices or the publisher. The two new tables hold English text only. `road()` reads a learner's own sessions and review logs, which already exist and are already in their data export and deleted with the account. No new learner table is added, so the export and deletion tests are unchanged.

**What the system refuses, even if asked nicely.**
- Publishing a dialogue with no approval file entry that matches its current fingerprint, or with a failing check. There is no override switch.
- Reporting the register check as passed when the judge did not run.
- Any AI call from the import, the voices or the fix step when the judge is off.
- Returning a stop's review words the learner has not met.
- Voicing past 90 percent of the month's free allowance.
- Sending learner data to a voice or AI service.
- Writing to or reading from any database other than through migrations and the test database.
- Printing or logging a key.

## Decision

| # | Choice | Pick | Runner up | Why the runner up lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where the road lives | Two new tables, `road_parts` and `road_stops`, plus `units.stop_number` | Part and stop columns on `units`, read through a `security definer` function | Units are hidden until approved, so the road could not list locked stops without a function that bypasses the access rules. Job 9 and 18 use `security invoker`; the new tables hold English only, so a plain read rule is enough |
| 2 | Stop and part names | Parsed from the owner's own markdown, with a test against drift | A second hand typed JSON copy | Two copies drift. The owner already approved the markdown names |
| 3 | How written dialogues become drafts | A deterministic importer, no AI | Having the pipeline's AI re-link and write "why" lines for Claude's text | It costs about $7 for the road (job 7's log: $0.04 to $0.07 per 6 line dialogue for the "why" call, scaled to 8 lines, my arithmetic) and the owner asked for no paid calls. Kept as a switch only for the judge |
| 4 | Register check without the AI judge | Off by default, recorded as "not run", switchable (owner's call 2) | A "judge" pass done by Claude in a working session | The same author checking its own work is not independent, and it would look like a passed check. Offered only as option C of call 2 |
| 5 | Mapping spoken to written words | Order plus an accepted pair table and endings rules, each failure named | An edit distance score | A score would accept `kolme` for `kolmea` and cannot explain a lexical swap such as `joo` for `kyllä`. A listed pair is checkable and shows the reviewer every register difference |
| 6 | Level fit for hand written content | The cumulative catalogue, plus a short recorded list of allowed extras per stop | Lowering the threshold, or skipping the level check | Thresholds are the owner's PRD numbers and the AGENTS rule forbids weakening a check. A recorded extra is visible and counted in the report |
| 7 | Word card meanings | One column per text on `forms` (`meaning_en`, `lemma_meaning_en`) | A `lemma_glosses` table | Forms already carry approval, freezing, withdrawal and a reference. A table would need its own copy of those rules. Consistency per dictionary form is a test instead |
| 8 | Voices | Azure fi-FI voices through the existing REST call, throttled to the free tier's 20 requests a minute | Azure batch synthesis | Batch synthesis is not available on the free tier (Azure quotas page) |
| 9 | Publish migrations | One per dialogue, reusing job 7's publisher and its second run test | One per part | A part file is large and cannot be proven twice per dialogue. Cost: 108 files and a few megabytes of SQL (my estimate, measured in `build.md`) |
| 10 | Owner approval record | A committed file per part, bound to fingerprints, quoted line included | A plain `approval_ref` text with no file | Nothing could later show what was approved or detect a changed line |
| 11 | How reviewer fixes return | Into the markdown first, then re-import and a fix migration | Patching `draft.json` only | The markdown is the source. A patched draft would be overwritten by the next import, or the drift test would fail |
| 12 | Where the review lesson is recorded | Derived from existing review logs (statement 5) | A new session type or table | A new learner table needs export, deletion and rule changes for no gain. The derived rule holds under either answer to owner's call 4 |
| 13 | Meaning of "a stop's new forms" | Forms first met in that stop (about 12 new words a dialogue, so about 36 a stop) | Every form in the stop's three dialogues | Repeated forms would swell the lesson to roughly 90 cards and ask old words again out of schedule |

Providers are job 7's: Azure fi-FI neural voices, provisional until the risk tests. No new provider is added.

## Risks and flags

- **Look closely: Finnish learning content live without a native speaker.** This is the owner's decision of 1 November. The automatic checks prove the written Finnish is real, in level and consistent with Voikko. They cannot tell whether the spoken Finnish sounds natural, and without the judge nothing automatic compares the two registers beyond the word mapping. Nothing checks the English glosses at all. Lexical swaps (`joo`, `kyllä`; `Ei mitään`, `Ei kestä`) are accepted by a table Claude wrote. The owner's approval therefore rests on the check numbers and a sample, and the reviewer is the real guard.
- **Look closely: paid services.** None by default. If the judge is on: about $0.60 on the owner's Anthropic key. Azure stays inside the free tier by a guard.
- **Look closely: learner data.** `road()` and `/api/road` read a learner's own sessions and review logs. Two learner pgTAP tests cover them. No new learner table.
- **Look closely: database access rules.** Two new tables, one changed approval check, two functions. Each is `revoke`d by default, granted to `authenticated` only and tested per condition.
- **Existing tests may break once real content exists.** Statement 23 is the answer. The risk is real: every throwaway database and the test project will hold units 1 to 36 before the fixtures.
- **Silent truncation at 1,000 rows.** A learner who has met every dialogue has about 10,000 tokens and up to about 1,500 cards. Any read that selects them without paging is cut by the API's row cap without an error.
- **Unknown failure rate.** Claude wrote 102 dialogues with no Voikko run. The first import may reject many lines. Known examples: 37 spoken words glued to `sä`; lines where the written version drops a pronoun; `Just!` against `Juuri niin!` where one written word has no spoken partner; names and compounds such as `MobilePay`, `HSL-sovelluksesta` and `AB-lippu`. The repair rule (14) and its 5 percent stop line manage it.
- **Spoken audio may sound wrong.** No voice provider claims to handle spoken Finnish (risk test 2). The reviewer marks clips; native recordings cost money and would be a later call. If risk test 2 changes the provider, every clip is remade.
- **Spoken spelling change.** About 37 words change to two words (call 5). It stays consistent with the six earlier drafts.
- **Repository size.** About 30 MB of audio twice (drafts and `public/audio`) is my guess from 48 kbit/s mono and about 3 seconds a clip; the stop line is 100 MB in total.
- **Reviewer workload.** 36 to 54 hours is large for one person. Reviewing the road part by part, in the order learners reach it, is the natural plan; it is the owner's to arrange.
- **Merge order with job 19.** Both may touch `docs/data-model.md`, `docs/conventions.md` and `src/lib/database.types.ts`. Types come only from the CLI. Whoever merges second regenerates them.
- **Fake approval files.** A process rule, not a technical lock: only the manager adds files to `content/approvals/`, on the owner's written line, and the summary lists each file with its date for the owner to confirm.
- **Names of unpublished stops are visible** to every signed in learner through `road_stops`. The same text is in the public repository.
- **Processing outside the EU.** None unless the judge is on; then briefs go to Anthropic's global service. They hold only game content, no learner data.
